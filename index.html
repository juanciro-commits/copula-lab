How to read the three plots

1. Original scale. The pairs as observed. Above and to the side, the distributions chosen for X and Y. Pearson is computed here and depends on those shapes.

2. Uniforms. Each variable passed through its own cdf: u = F(x), v = G(y). The histograms are always flat; all that remains is how the points pair up. This cloud is the copula.

3. Normal scale. The same copula under a magnifying glass: z = Φ⁻¹(u). The Gaussian gives ellipses; the other families bend the ellipse in their tails.
